Furnace Not Turning On

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

A rotten-egg smell only when the hot water runs is a water problem. Use the rotten-egg smell page.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

If you smell burning plastic or see smoke or sparks, use the burning smell steps and stop. If someone in the home is a baby, an older adult, or has a medical need and the home is getting cold, get them warm first. A website check comes second.

Causes, most common first

  1. The thermostat isn’t calling for heat. Wrong mode, a setpoint at or below the room, or the fan left On.
  2. A clogged filter or a blocked return. The furnace can shut itself off. You can check a filter you reach from the floor.
  3. The furnace switch or a tripped breaker. Look only. A furnace breaker is not reset.
  4. Snow, ice, or debris on the outside vent pipes, or a kinked condensate tube.
  5. A code or a blinking light. Read it through the window. Don’t open the door to find it.
  6. No gas, no propane, or no oil. A stove that won’t light, or a tank gauge you can already see, is a clue. Don’t turn a gas valve.

Checks you can do

  1. Look at the thermostat you already use. Don’t take it off the wall. Set mode to Heat, fan to Auto, and the setpoint a few degrees above the room temperature. Fan On blows room-temperature air between heating cycles, which can feel cold. Some thermostats wait a few minutes before they start.
  2. Turn the system Off at the thermostat and wait until the blower stops. That does not cut power. Use only a tool-free filter slot or return grille you can reach from the floor. Read the size printed on the filter frame, put in one filter of that size, and point the airflow arrow toward the furnace. Close the slot. Don’t open a burner door to find a filter. Details are on the filter size page.
  3. Find the switch that looks like a light switch on or near the furnace. Look only. If it was turned off on purpose, or you aren’t sure, leave it off. The guided check is the place that walks through turning that switch back on, and only once, when it was switched off by mistake and there is no smell, water, or noise.
  4. Furnace breakers are never reset. Look at the breaker marked for the furnace. If the handle is in the middle or off, leave it and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or a disconnect box.
  5. If a light is blinking or a display shows a code, don’t turn the power off. Cutting power can erase the code on some furnaces. Count the flashes for a full minute or photograph the display, then use the blinking light page.
  6. On a high-efficiency furnace, two plastic pipes on an outside wall bring air in and send exhaust out. From the ground, brush snow and leaves away from both ends by hand or with a soft broom, and clear a wide area around them. Leave ice that is stuck to a pipe. Stop if a pipe is cracked, loose, pulled apart, or has ice inside it.
  7. A high-efficiency furnace makes water. A clear or white tube outside the cabinet carries it to a floor drain or a small pump. Look from outside the furnace. If a tube you can reach without tools is kinked, gently straighten it by hand. Don’t pull it off. Stop if the tube is frozen, cracked, or you would have to open the furnace.
  8. On propane or oil, read the tank gauge you can already see. Don’t adjust a valve, and don’t press a red reset button on an oil burner.

It’s not normal if

The furnace starts and stops every few minutes, you hear a bang when it tries to light, the vent pipe is damaged, water is near a switch or outlet, or the same code comes back. Short cycling has its own page. Water near electrical parts means stay out of it and call. Don’t mop toward the panel.

Call a pro for these

Don’t open the furnace, relight a pilot, turn a gas valve, or bypass a safety switch so it keeps running. Burners, the flame sensor, the igniter, the control board, and refrigerant lines are pro work. If you hear hissing at refrigerant pipes, turn the system Off at the thermostat and don’t touch the pipes, valves, or caps.

If the blower runs and the air stays cool, use blowing cold air. If this is a boiler or radiators, use boiler not heating instead. A heat pump that heats with the outdoor unit belongs on the heat-pump check, not this one.

Start the guided check and choose Furnace or air handler, then No heat: nothing seems to happen.

Boiler or Radiators Not Heating

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

A rotten-egg smell only when the hot water runs is a water problem. Use the rotten-egg smell page.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

If hot water or steam is spraying or pouring, or a relief pipe is discharging, stay back. Don’t touch a valve or try to plug the pipe. Call a licensed heating professional. For a serious burn, call 911.

If someone is a baby, an older adult, or has a medical need and they don’t have enough heat, get them warm first.

Causes, most common first

  1. The thermostat isn’t calling. Mode isn’t Heat, or the setpoint is at or below the room. Some homes have a thermostat for each zone.
  2. A switch or breaker is off. Look only. Don’t flip it.
  3. A code or lockout on the display. Write it down. Don’t clear it.
  4. Furniture or covers against the baseboard or radiators.
  5. Air in the pipes, a closed zone valve, or a boiler that isn’t firing. Those need a pro. This page does not include bleeding steps.

Checks you can do

  • Don’t take the thermostat off the wall. Set Heat and a setpoint a few degrees above the room. Radiators and floors can take a while to warm. If that room has its own thermostat, check that one too.
  • At the boiler, look only. Many boilers have a power switch and a service switch nearby, often with a red plate. Don’t flip any switch or breaker, and don’t open the boiler. If a switch is off or a breaker looks tripped, leave it and call.
  • Write down a code exactly. Don’t open the jacket to read a chart.
  • If you can already read the gauge, write the number down. If it sits on a relief setting or warning mark identified for your boiler, don’t touch the boiler. Call a pro.
  • On a steam boiler, if the sight glass shows no water, don’t add water. Call a pro.
  • Move furniture, curtains, and covers off the cold baseboard or radiators, then give it time.
  • Don’t turn a gas valve, relight a pilot, or press a red reset button on an oil burner.

When some radiators stay cold

Gurgling, or a radiator that is warm at the bottom and cold at the top, can mean air in a hot-water system or a steam vent that isn’t working. Both are reasons to call a licensed heating professional. This page does not include radiator or baseboard bleeding steps.

It’s not normal if

You hear a bang or boom when the boiler starts, you see soot or scorch marks, or flame where the burner area shouldn’t show it. Stop using the boiler and call. Don’t open a door to inspect the flame.

If the heat is forced air from a furnace, use furnace not turning on instead.

Start the guided check and choose Something else, then Boiler, radiators, baseboard, or radiant floors, then No heat anywhere.

No Heat in an Apartment

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

A rotten-egg smell only when the hot water runs is a water problem. Use the rotten-egg smell page.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

If someone is a baby, an older adult, or has a medical need and the apartment is getting cold, get them warm first. That can mean another room, a neighbor, or somewhere else. Don’t wait on a reply if people are at risk.

What you can check without opening the building’s equipment

  • If you have a thermostat in the apartment, set Heat, fan Auto, and a setpoint a few degrees above the room. Don’t take it off the wall. Don’t open a locked mechanical closet, a roof unit, or a boiler room.
  • Look at supply vents and radiators in your unit. Move your own furniture off a vent or a baseboard. Don’t bleed a radiator. That can dump water into the apartment, and this site doesn’t give bleeding steps.
  • Don’t reset a breaker in a building panel, and don’t open the panel cover. If your own unit’s filter is in a tool-free slot you can reach from the floor, the filter page applies. If the filter is in a locked room, leave it.
  • Write down a code you can see without removing a cover. Don’t clear it.

What to send the landlord or manager

Housing rules differ by city and state. This page is not legal advice, and it does not say you should withhold rent. Ask in writing, and keep a copy. Include the date, the thermostat settings you used, whether any air or radiators get warm, and whether other apartments in the building have heat if you know that. If you smelled gas, say that you left and called the gas utility or 911. Don’t stay inside to finish the message.

A local housing or health office is the place that knows the no-heat complaint process where you live. 211 can help you find that office. Bring the written note.

While you wait

Never heat with an oven, stove, grill, charcoal, or generator indoors. Those can fill a room with carbon monoxide. CPSC winter weather tips. An electric space heater only helps if the building allows it and the outlet can take it. Use the space heater page. Don’t run a generator on a balcony or in a hallway.

If the apartment has its own furnace and you can reach it from your rooms, the furnace not turning on checks apply. If the heat is a boiler or radiators, use boiler not heating. Stop at any step that means opening equipment you don’t control.

Start the guided check and choose the equipment you can already identify. If you can’t, choose Furnace or air handler only for a unit you can see in your own space.

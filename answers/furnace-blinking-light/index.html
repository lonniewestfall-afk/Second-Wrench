Furnace Blinking Light or Error Code

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

A blinking light or a code means the furnace shut itself off or is warning you. Don’t clear it to make the light go steady. The code is what you tell the pro.

Read it without opening the furnace

  1. Most furnaces have a small window in the lower door, or a display, or a code on the thermostat. Look only. Don’t remove the door. On some brands, cutting power or taking the door off erases the stored code.
  2. Watch for one full minute. Codes repeat, with a pause between them. Count short flashes and long flashes separately. A phone video helps.
  3. Write the brand name printed on the front of the furnace. Codes are not the same from one brand to the next, or even from one board to the next.
  4. If a chart is printed where you can read it without removing a door, and it disagrees with something you read online, trust the chart on the furnace. If the chart is inside the door, leave the door on and give the blink count to a licensed HVAC professional.
  5. Don’t flip the breaker to “reset the code.” Furnace breakers are never reset. Look at the breaker marked for the furnace. If the handle is in the middle or off, leave it and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or a disconnect box.

What you can check around the code

A blocked vent or a condensate problem is a common reason a high-efficiency furnace locks out. On a high-efficiency furnace, two plastic pipes on an outside wall bring air in and send exhaust out. From the ground, brush snow and leaves away from both ends by hand or with a soft broom, and clear a wide area around them. Leave ice that is stuck to a pipe. Stop if a pipe is cracked, loose, pulled apart, or has ice inside it. A high-efficiency furnace makes water. A clear or white tube outside the cabinet carries it to a floor drain or a small pump. Look from outside the furnace. If a tube you can reach without tools is kinked, gently straighten it by hand. Don’t pull it off. Stop if the tube is frozen, cracked, or you would have to open the furnace. Then wait and see if the furnace starts on its own. If the same code returns, stop. Don’t keep cycling power.

Also check the filter and the thermostat, using the filter page and Heat / Auto / setpoint above the room. Those don’t clear a code by themselves. They remove two reasons the furnace may have shut off.

Stop, don’t interpret it yourself, if

  • You smell gas, exhaust, or burning, or a CO alarm sounds.
  • The code comes back after the vents and the drain are clear.
  • You see soot, scorch marks, or you heard a bang when it tried to start.
  • The only way to see the code is to open a panel or a service menu that asks for a password.

This page does not list brand blink charts. A wrong chart can send you into the burner or the board. An outdoor heat pump or side-discharge unit uses a different code page: side-discharge error code.

Start the guided check and choose Furnace or air handler, then A light is blinking or a code is showing on the furnace.

Is This HVAC Quote Too High? How to Get a Second Opinion

Last updated October 29, 2026.

A quote can look high and still match the work, or look low and leave out a permit, a thermostat, or haul-away. This page does not price the job and does not say what a quote should cost. It lists what to get in writing so a second company can look at the same job.

Before anyone comes

Write down what you already know: thermostat mode and setpoint, whether the filter looks dirty, any code you can see without opening a door, and what the system does (nothing, cold air, short cycling, a noise). Start the guided check and choose Furnace or air handler. That note is for you to read to the company. It is not a diagnosis.

What the written quote should name

  • The equipment model, or the repair part, and whether it is a repair or a replacement.
  • Labor, permit, haul-away of the old equipment, a new thermostat, and any duct changes. Ask them to list what is not included.
  • The warranty they are offering, and who you call if it fails. Your existing warranty may require a licensed pro. Check it before you approve work.
  • What they say failed, and what they looked at. A quote that never mentions the thermostat, the filter, or the outside vents skipped the first checks on these pages.
  • Whether a repair was considered, and why they moved on from it. You can ask for that in writing. You don’t have to accept the answer on the spot.

Getting a second look

  1. Give the second company the same description of the symptom. Don’t hand them only the first company’s price and ask them to beat it.
  2. Ask both to separate what they observed from what they recommend.
  3. Compare the scope, not a single bottom line. Two quotes for different equipment are not the same job.
  4. Gas valves, burners, refrigerant, and live electrical work belong to a licensed pro. Ask who holds the license for that part of the job. Don’t agree to do those steps yourself to shrink the quote.
  5. If you are pressed to sign the same day, or the only reason given is that the equipment is “done” with nothing written down, get the second look before you approve.

What this page will not do

It will not tell you the quote is too high or too low. It will not tell you to repair or replace. A cracked heat exchanger, a refrigerant leak, or a failed board is something the pro has to show you a reason for. Refrigerant work needs an EPA-certified technician. Don’t ask anyone to bypass a safety to keep the old unit running until you decide.

If the bill is the reason you’re considering a replacement, start with why a heating bill runs high. A dirty filter or Emergency Heat can raise a bill without a failed furnace.

Start the guided check and choose the equipment you actually have, then the symptom that matches.

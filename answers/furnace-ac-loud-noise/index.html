Furnace or AC Making a Loud Noise

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

If you smell burning, see smoke or sparks, or hear grinding metal, turn the system Off at the thermostat and call a licensed HVAC professional. If there is smoke or fire, get everyone outside and call 911. Don’t open a panel to find the noise, and don’t keep the system running so you can hear it better.

Match the sound

  • Bang or boom when the furnace starts. Stop using the furnace and call. That can be delayed ignition. Don’t open the burner door, and don’t relight anything.
  • Hum or buzz at the outdoor unit while the fan is not spinning. Stay outside the grille. Don’t push the fan with a stick. This often points at a capacitor or contactor, which is pro work. Advanced repairs are off on this site. Call a licensed HVAC professional.
  • Rattle from the outdoor cabinet. From dry ground, look for a leaf, a twig, or something leaning on the cabinet. Don’t reach through the grille and don’t touch the fins. Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.
  • Whistle or a high whoosh indoors. Check the filter and whether a return is blocked or a lot of supply vents are closed. A filter in backward or two filters stacked can whistle and then shut the system off.
  • Clicking, then silence, over and over. That can be short cycling. Use the short cycling page instead of restarting it.
  • Banging pipes or radiators. Use boiler not heating. Don’t bleed a radiator from these instructions.
  • Hissing at refrigerant pipes. Turn the system Off at the thermostat. Don’t touch the pipes, valves, or caps. Refrigerant work needs an EPA-certified technician.

Breakers

Furnace breakers are never reset. Look at the breaker marked for the furnace. If the handle is in the middle or off, leave it and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or a disconnect box. A heat pump breaker is not reset from this page either. A cooling-only outdoor unit has a separate one-time breaker step, and only inside the guided check, when that check offers it. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box. If water is near the outdoor disconnect, stay back and call.

Ice and snow

Don’t chip ice off a coil or a fan. On a heat pump, a cloud of steam for a few minutes in cold weather can be defrost. Ice that is still there after about an hour of running is not normal. Clear loose snow from the ground around the unit. Leave ice that is frozen to the cabinet.

For a furnace noise, Start the guided check and choose Furnace or air handler, then An unusual noise. For a cooling-only air conditioner, Start the guided check and choose Cooling-only AC, then Unusual noise. For a heat pump, Start the guided check and choose Heat pump.

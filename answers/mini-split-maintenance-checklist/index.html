Mini-split maintenance checklist

Last updated October 29, 2026.

A ductless mini-split has an indoor unit on a wall, floor, or ceiling and a small outdoor unit. These checks stay outside the cabinets. Cleaning the coil inside the indoor unit is not on this page.

Remote or app

Before a filter check, turn the unit off with the remote or app and wait until the fan stops. That stops it running. It does not cut power. Don’t climb on a chair or ladder to reach the indoor unit.

Indoor filters

Most mini-splits have washable filters behind a front cover that lifts by hand. Clean them only when your owner’s manual shows the steps without tools, the cover lifts without prying or unscrewing, and you can reach it standing on the floor. If the manual says to switch power off at a breaker or disconnect first, use this wording and stop if it asks for anything else:

Turn the system Off at the thermostat. Then switch off its breaker (or the outdoor disconnect or service switch near the unit). When you're done and everything is dry, switch it back on. If a breaker trips again or keeps tripping, leave it off and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box.

Rinse filters with water only if the manual says to, away from the electrical parts. Let them dry, put them back, and close the cover before you turn the unit on. If the cover needs tools, force, or a ladder, leave it for a licensed HVAC professional.

Water from the indoor unit

A slow drip away from outlets can be a clogged condensate drain. Put down a towel and call a pro to clear the drain. If water reaches a cord, outlet, or switch, stay clear and don’t touch the unit or the breaker panel.

Outdoor unit

Keep plants, snow, and stored items off the intake and the discharge. Don’t reach through the grille. Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil. Don’t reset a mini-split breaker to test it. If a breaker looks tripped, leave it and call a licensed HVAC professional or electrician. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box.

Pick a time you’ll remember. Your manual sets how often.

Warranty note: Your warranty may require professional maintenance or product registration. Check your warranty and your owner’s manual before you do a task yourself. Some makers require service records or work by a licensed pro. When in doubt, ask your installer or the maker. Following a checklist here does not keep, protect, or extend a warranty.

Outdoor coil cleaning

Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.

Trane, Daikin, and Amana

Your manual says not to clean this unit with water because of the risk of electric shock or damage. Don't hose or spray it. Leave coil cleaning to a licensed HVAC professional.

No water or chemical steps are shown for Trane, Daikin, or Amana.

Carrier

Turn the system Off at the thermostat. Then switch off its breaker (or the outdoor disconnect or service switch near the unit). When you're done and everything is dry, switch it back on. If a breaker trips again or keeps tripping, leave it off and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box.

Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.

If you use a coil cleaner, use one sold for home AC coils and follow its label exactly: mixing, how long it sits, rinsing, gloves and eye protection. Keep it off plants, pets and the electrical parts. Never mix cleaners.

Use a garden hose with a gentle spray or a pump sprayer, from outside the grille. Don't brush, comb or touch the fins. Don't spray into the electrical box or the fan motor.

Bosch, Goodman, Lennox, and Rheem

Your owner's manual recommends a qualified technician for this cleaning. You can choose to do it yourself, but it's your call.

Furnace Smells Like Burning the First Time You Turn On the Heat

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

A rotten-egg smell only when the hot water runs is a water problem. Use the rotten-egg smell page.

If you can see smoke, or a smoke alarm is going off, get everyone outside now and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn the furnace off.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

What the smell is telling you

  1. Dusty or slightly burnt, and it started the first time the heat ran this season. Dust on a heat exchanger can smell while it warms up. In the guided check, that path is for a smell that is light and fading, and the heat has run less than about an hour.
  2. Burning plastic, rubber, or an electrical smell. That is not a dust smell. Shut the furnace off with the steps below and call a licensed HVAC professional.
  3. The dusty smell lasts about an hour or longer, or it isn’t fading. Treat it the same as a plastic or electrical smell. Shut it off and call.
  4. A bang, soot, or scorch marks. Stop using the furnace. Don’t open a door to look at the flame.

If you shut it off

Turn the heat Off at the thermostat now. Then switch off the furnace’s service switch or its breaker (switch it Off only). Never open the panel cover. Don’t reset a breaker that trips again. Don’t run the heat again until a licensed HVAC professional has checked it. If you see smoke or the smell gets stronger, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside.

If the house is getting cold, call a licensed HVAC professional. If it’s dangerously cold and anyone is at risk, get them somewhere warm first. Never heat with an oven, stove, grill, charcoal, or generator indoors. Those can fill a room with carbon monoxide. CPSC winter weather tips. If you use an electric space heater, plug it straight into a wall outlet. Don’t use a power strip or extension cord, and don’t run the cord under a rug or carpet. Keep all sides at least three feet from beds, clothes, curtains, papers, and furniture. Put it on a stable, level surface where it won’t be knocked over. Don’t leave it running unattended in a confined space. CPSC.

After a dusty smell has faded

Only if the smell was light, faded inside about an hour, and there is no smoke: check the filter and make sure returns aren’t blocked. Don’t open the furnace to vacuum the heat exchanger or the burners. Those parts stay closed.

Don’t do these

  • Don’t keep the heat running “to burn the smell off” once it has lasted about an hour, gotten stronger, or smells like plastic, rubber, or wiring.
  • Don’t open the furnace, pull a board, or spray anything into the cabinet.
  • Don’t reset a furnace breaker to see if the smell goes away.
  • Don’t bypass a limit or a rollout switch. Those switches turned the furnace off for a reason.

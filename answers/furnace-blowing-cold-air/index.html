Furnace Running but Blowing Cold Air

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

A rotten-egg smell only when the hot water runs is a water problem. Use the rotten-egg smell page.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

Two different problems get lumped together. The blower can be running while the air stays cool, or the vents can be quiet because the blower isn’t running. The checks overlap, and the ending doesn’t.

Air is moving, and it isn’t warm

  1. Look at the thermostat you already use. Don’t take it off the wall. Set mode to Heat, fan to Auto, and the setpoint a few degrees above the room temperature. Fan On blows room-temperature air between heating cycles, which can feel cold. Some thermostats wait a few minutes before they start. Wait one full heating cycle before you decide the air is cold. The blower often starts before the burner, and the first minute can feel cool.
  2. If this is a heat pump, a few minutes of cool air in cold weather can be defrost. Steam at the outdoor unit for a few minutes can be normal. If there is still no heat after about an hour, or ice stays on the outdoor unit, call a licensed HVAC professional. Don’t chip the ice. Emergency Heat usually costs more to run. Don’t leave it on as the everyday setting. Start the guided check and choose Heat pump, then the no-heat path.
  3. Turn the system Off at the thermostat and wait until the blower stops. That does not cut power. Use only a tool-free filter slot or return grille you can reach from the floor. Read the size printed on the filter frame, put in one filter of that size, and point the airflow arrow toward the furnace. Close the slot. Don’t open a burner door to find a filter.
  4. Open supply vents and uncover return grilles. A system can blow hard from one vent and almost nothing from another if the others are closed or a duct has come loose. Don’t crawl a tight attic to reseat a duct. If you can see a duct joint that has fallen open in a basement you already walk through, leave it and call. Tape is not a repair this page teaches.
  5. If the blower keeps running and the air is still cool after a full cycle, the burner or the heat strips are not heating. Don’t open the furnace to look at the flame or the elements. Call a licensed HVAC professional. Give them the thermostat settings, whether you smell anything, and any blink code you could see through the window.

No air from the vents

  1. Confirm the thermostat is on Heat, not Off, and the fan isn’t stuck in a mode the screen doesn’t explain. If the screen is blank, use smart thermostat no power.
  2. Check the filter. A packed filter can leave the vents feeling dead.
  3. Look at the blower door. If it isn’t seated, some furnaces won’t run the blower. Don’t remove the door. Push it shut only if you can do that without tools and without reaching inside.
  4. Look at the furnace switch and the breaker. Furnace breakers are never reset. Look at the breaker marked for the furnace. If the handle is in the middle or off, leave it and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or a disconnect box.
  5. On a high-efficiency furnace, two plastic pipes on an outside wall bring air in and send exhaust out. From the ground, brush snow and leaves away from both ends by hand or with a soft broom, and clear a wide area around them. Leave ice that is stuck to a pipe. Stop if a pipe is cracked, loose, pulled apart, or has ice inside it. A blocked exhaust can keep the furnace from firing, and then there is no warm air. The blower may or may not run, depending on the furnace.
  6. A high-efficiency furnace makes water. A clear or white tube outside the cabinet carries it to a floor drain or a small pump. Look from outside the furnace. If a tube you can reach without tools is kinked, gently straighten it by hand. Don’t pull it off. Stop if the tube is frozen, cracked, or you would have to open the furnace.

Don’t do these

  • Don’t open the burner compartment or the electrical panel.
  • Don’t turn a gas valve or relight a pilot to “get the flame back.”
  • Don’t bypass a limit switch because the furnace starts and then stops. That’s short cycling.
  • Don’t set the fan to On and leave it there to force air. You will feel a steady cool breeze and the heat may still be off.

Start the guided check and choose Furnace or air handler, then Blower runs, but the air isn’t warm. If nothing is coming out of the vents, choose No heat: nothing seems to happen instead.

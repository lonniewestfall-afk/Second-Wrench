Geothermal heat pump maintenance checklist

Last updated October 29, 2026.

A geothermal or water-source heat pump sits indoors, often in a basement or closet. Water or loop fluid comes in through pipes. There is no outdoor fan unit to hose down. These checks stay outside the cabinet.

Warranty note: Your warranty may require professional maintenance or product registration. Check your warranty and your owner’s manual before you do a task yourself. Some makers require service records or work by a licensed pro. When in doubt, ask your installer or the maker. Following a checklist here does not keep, protect, or extend a warranty.

Pick a time you’ll remember. Your manual sets how often.

Thermostat and filter

Set the mode you mean to use and leave Emergency Heat off unless a pro told you to use it while you wait. Turn the system Off at the thermostat and wait for the blower to stop. Change the filter only in a tool-free slot you can reach from the floor, one filter, arrow toward the blower. Don’t open the cabinet to find a filter. ENERGY STAR says to check a filter every month in heavy-use seasons and change it at least every 3 months, or when it looks dirty. Your manual wins if it differs.

Condensate and water

Look at a condensate tube you can already see outside the cabinet. Straighten a kink by hand only if you don’t need tools and you don’t pull the tube off. If water is on or near wiring, an outlet, or the breaker panel, stay out of it and call a licensed HVAC professional. Don’t touch the panel.

What stays with a pro

  • Loop fluid, antifreeze, well water, and any chemical treatment. Don’t add anything to the loop.
  • Refrigerant. If you hear hissing, turn the system Off at the thermostat. Don’t touch the pipes, valves, or caps.
  • Opening the cabinet, resetting a breaker, or working on live wiring. Never open the breaker panel cover.
  • A loop gauge you cannot already read from where you stand. If you can read it, write the number down and give it to your pro. Don’t adjust a valve to change it.

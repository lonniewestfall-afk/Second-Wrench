AC maintenance checklist

Last updated October 29, 2026.

These are look-and-check tasks between visits from a licensed HVAC professional. Outdoor coil cleaning is below, split by brand. Other repairs stay with your owner’s manual.

Thermostat

Set the system to Off at the thermostat and wait until the blower stops before you open a filter slot. This does not disconnect electricity. Don’t take the thermostat off the wall.

Air filter

Use only a tool-free filter slot or return grille your owner’s manual names, reachable from the floor. Keep furnace and air-handler doors closed. The size is printed on the filter’s frame. Put one filter back with the airflow arrow pointing toward the blower, and close the slot. ENERGY STAR says to check the filter every month in heavy-use seasons and to change it at least every 3 months, or sooner when it looks dirty. Your manual wins if it says something different.

Condensate drain

Look at the drain tube or pan you can already see, outside the cabinet. If a tube you can reach without tools is kinked, gently straighten it by hand. Don’t pull it off. If water is near a switch, outlet, or the breaker panel, stay out of it and call a licensed HVAC professional. Don’t open the panel cover.

Outdoor unit

From dry ground, move leaves, toys, and stored items away from the cabinet. Don’t reach through the grille or touch the fins. Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil. If the outdoor breaker looks tripped, leave it and use the guided check. That check is the only place a one-time outdoor reset is offered, and only for a cooling-only air conditioner. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box.

Pick a time you’ll remember. Your manual sets how often.

Warranty note: Your warranty may require professional maintenance or product registration. Check your warranty and your owner’s manual before you do a task yourself. Some makers require service records or work by a licensed pro. When in doubt, ask your installer or the maker. Following a checklist here does not keep, protect, or extend a warranty.

Outdoor coil cleaning

Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.

Trane, Daikin, and Amana

Your manual says not to clean this unit with water because of the risk of electric shock or damage. Don't hose or spray it. Leave coil cleaning to a licensed HVAC professional.

No water or chemical steps are shown for Trane, Daikin, or Amana.

Carrier

Turn the system Off at the thermostat. Then switch off its breaker (or the outdoor disconnect or service switch near the unit). When you're done and everything is dry, switch it back on. If a breaker trips again or keeps tripping, leave it off and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box.

Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.

If you use a coil cleaner, use one sold for home AC coils and follow its label exactly: mixing, how long it sits, rinsing, gloves and eye protection. Keep it off plants, pets and the electrical parts. Never mix cleaners.

Use a garden hose with a gentle spray or a pump sprayer, from outside the grille. Don't brush, comb or touch the fins. Don't spray into the electrical box or the fan motor.

Bosch, Goodman, Lennox, and Rheem

Your owner's manual recommends a qualified technician for this cleaning. You can choose to do it yourself, but it's your call.

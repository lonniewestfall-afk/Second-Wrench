Heat pump maintenance checklist

Last updated October 29, 2026.

These are look-and-check tasks between visits from a licensed HVAC professional. Outdoor coil cleaning is below, split by brand. Refrigerant and electrical work stay with a pro.

Thermostat

In heating season, mode is Heat, not Emergency Heat, unless a pro told you to use Emergency Heat while you wait. Emergency or Aux heat usually costs more to run. Don’t take the thermostat off the wall. A large overnight setback on a heat pump can call on backup electric heat. The U.S. Department of Energy Energy Saver guide says not to set a heat pump back 7°–10°F unless the thermostat is made for heat pumps.

Air filter

Same rule as a furnace filter: thermostat Off, blower stopped, one filter, arrow toward the blower, tool-free slot you can reach from the floor. ENERGY STAR says check monthly in heavy-use seasons and change at least every 3 months, or when the filter looks dirty.

Snow, ice, and the outdoor unit

Brush loose snow off the ground around the unit. Don’t chip ice, don’t reach through the grille, and don’t try to turn the fan. In cold weather a cloud of steam during defrost can be normal and usually lasts a few minutes. Ice that is still there after about an hour of running is not normal. Call a licensed HVAC professional. Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.

Heat-pump breakers are not reset from this page. If a breaker looks tripped or a disconnect is off, leave it and call a licensed HVAC professional or electrician. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box.

Condensate

In cooling season, look at the indoor drain tube you can already see. Straighten a kink by hand only if the tube is outside the cabinet and you don’t need tools. Stop if water is near electrical parts.

Pick a time you’ll remember. Your manual sets how often.

Warranty note: Your warranty may require professional maintenance or product registration. Check your warranty and your owner’s manual before you do a task yourself. Some makers require service records or work by a licensed pro. When in doubt, ask your installer or the maker. Following a checklist here does not keep, protect, or extend a warranty.

Outdoor coil cleaning

Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.

Trane, Daikin, and Amana

Your manual says not to clean this unit with water because of the risk of electric shock or damage. Don't hose or spray it. Leave coil cleaning to a licensed HVAC professional.

No water or chemical steps are shown for Trane, Daikin, or Amana.

Carrier

Turn the system Off at the thermostat. Then switch off its breaker (or the outdoor disconnect or service switch near the unit). When you're done and everything is dry, switch it back on. If a breaker trips again or keeps tripping, leave it off and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover or the disconnect box.

Never use a pressure washer on your outdoor unit. It can flatten the fins and damage the coil.

If you use a coil cleaner, use one sold for home AC coils and follow its label exactly: mixing, how long it sits, rinsing, gloves and eye protection. Keep it off plants, pets and the electrical parts. Never mix cleaners.

Use a garden hose with a gentle spray or a pump sprayer, from outside the grille. Don't brush, comb or touch the fins. Don't spray into the electrical box or the fan motor.

Bosch, Goodman, Lennox, and Rheem

Your owner's manual recommends a qualified technician for this cleaning. You can choose to do it yourself, but it's your call.

Furnace maintenance checklist

Last updated October 29, 2026.

Do this on a mild day, before you need the heat. It is a look-and-check list. It does not open the furnace, relight a pilot, or turn a gas valve.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones until you are outside. A rotten-egg smell only from the hot water is a different problem: hot water smells like rotten eggs.

Warranty note: Your warranty may require professional maintenance or product registration. Check your warranty and your owner’s manual before you do a task yourself. Some makers require service records or work by a licensed pro. When in doubt, ask your installer or the maker. Following a checklist here does not keep, protect, or extend a warranty.

Pick a time you’ll remember. Your manual sets how often.

Alarms

Press the test button on each carbon monoxide alarm and smoke alarm. CPSC says to test them monthly. If a CO alarm sounds for real, get everyone outside and call 911. Don’t keep testing the furnace.

Thermostat

Don’t take it off the wall. Set mode to Heat, fan to Auto, and the setpoint a few degrees above the room temperature. Wait one full cycle. Fan On blows room-temperature air between cycles and can feel like the heat failed. Some thermostats wait a few minutes before they start.

Filter, returns, and supplies

Turn the system Off at the thermostat and wait until the blower stops. Use only a tool-free filter slot or return grille you can reach from the floor. Read the size on the old filter, put in one filter of that size, and point the arrow toward the furnace. Close the slot. Walk the house and make sure return grilles aren’t blocked by furniture and supply vents aren’t closed. Don’t open a burner door to find a filter. The filter size page has the longer version.

Outside vent pipes

A high-efficiency furnace has two plastic pipes on an outside wall. Brush snow, leaves, and shrub clippings away from both ends by hand or with a soft broom, from the ground. Leave ice that is stuck to a pipe. Stop if a pipe is cracked, loose, pulled apart, or full of ice, and call a licensed HVAC professional. CPSC says to clear snow from outside furnace vents so carbon monoxide does not build up in the house.

Condensate tube

Look at the clear or white tube outside the cabinet. If it is kinked and you can reach it without tools, gently straighten it by hand. Don’t pull it off. Stop if it is frozen, cracked, or you would have to open the furnace. If water is near a switch or outlet, stay out of it and call a pro. Don’t open the breaker panel.

Switch and breaker, look only

Many furnaces have a nearby switch that looks like a light switch. Look at it and at the breaker labeled for the furnace. Furnace breakers are never reset. If the breaker is in the middle or off, leave it and call a licensed HVAC professional. Never open the breaker panel cover. Don’t press a reset button on the furnace, and don’t open its doors.

What stays with a pro

  • Burners, the gas valve, the pilot, the igniter, and the flame sensor.
  • Anything inside the electrical compartment, including a control board.
  • A blinking code you cannot read without taking a door off. Write down what you can see through the window and use the blinking light page.
  • Refrigerant lines on a furnace that also has central air. If you hear hissing, turn the system Off at the thermostat and don’t touch the pipes, valves, or caps.

If the test run smells like burning, use the burning smell page before you leave the furnace running.

Gas Smell, Pilot Light Out, or a Furnace Flame That Looks Wrong

Last updated October 29, 2026.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs in the air, not just from the hot water, leave the house now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Don’t touch light switches or phones, or use lighters, matches, or anything that could spark or flame while you’re inside.

Gas utilities odorize natural gas so a leak smells like rotten eggs. Treat a gas smell, a hiss you can’t place, or a strong smell you can’t identify the same way: leave, then call from outside. Don’t flip switches on the way out.

A rotten-egg smell only when the hot water runs is a water problem. Use the rotten-egg smell page.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside. Don’t go back in to turn equipment off.

Pilot light out

Don’t relight a pilot. Don’t turn the gas valve to feed it, and don’t hold a button down to keep a flame lit. A pilot that won’t stay lit can be a thermocouple, a gas control, or a draft problem. Those are inside the combustion area. Call a licensed HVAC professional and leave the furnace off. The same rule is on the water-heater pages: relighting is a pro job here.

Flame color, soot, or a bang

Don’t open a door and don’t go look at the flame to judge it. If you have already seen a yellow or orange flame, soot or scorch marks, or flame outside the burner area, stop using the furnace or boiler. If you heard a bang or boom when it started, stop using it. Call a licensed pro. Don’t try to “adjust the air” on a burner. That is gas-valve and burner work.

What you may look at, only if you do not smell gas

  • A gas shut-off handle usually lines up with the pipe when it is open, and sits across the pipe when it is closed. Look. Don’t turn it.
  • On propane, read the tank gauge you can already see. Don’t open a tank valve to “wake up” the furnace.
  • Look at the thermostat you already use. Don’t take it off the wall. Set mode to Heat, fan to Auto, and the setpoint a few degrees above the room temperature. Fan On blows room-temperature air between heating cycles, which can feel cold. Some thermostats wait a few minutes before they start. Only if there is no gas smell and no CO alarm.
  • A blinking light you can see through the window. Don’t clear it by cutting power. Use the blinking light page.

Hard stops

Refrigerant valves stay closed. Don’t bypass a flame rollout switch, a spill switch, or a pressure switch so the furnace will run. Don’t open the electrical panel. If the vent pipe is cracked, disconnected, or rusted through, stop using the furnace and call. Don’t tape a flue back together.

Start the guided check and choose Furnace or air handler. The first furnace screen is the combustion and carbon monoxide check. For a boiler, Start the guided check and choose Something else, then Boiler, radiators, baseboard, or radiant floors.
